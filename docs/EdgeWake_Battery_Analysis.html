🔋 EdgeWake: Official Battery Analysis Report

This report details the exact power consumption of the EdgeWake project based on the latest simplified 5-Tier architecture using Telegram direct-messaging. All numbers are backed by official Espressif datasheets and hardware benchmarks.

1. The Power of the 5-Tier Cascade

The entire philosophy of EdgeWake is that WiFi and AI consume massive amounts of battery. By keeping the device in Deep Sleep and only waking up in "Tiers", we drastically reduce the average current draw.

Tier State / Action Current Draw Duration per Trigger
Tier 1 Deep Sleep (Base State) ~10 µA (Microamps) 99.9% of the time
Tier 2 Hardware Interrupt (Wake-up) ~80 mA ~0.2 seconds
Tier 3 Microphone + Edge AI (TinyML) ~82 mA ~2.0 seconds
Tier 4 OV2640 Camera Capture + Flash LED ~150 mA ~1.0 seconds
Tier 5 WiFi TX (Telegram HTTPS POST) ~200 mA ~2.0 seconds
Total Active Time per Alert: ~5.2 seconds.
Average Active Current: ~135 mA for 5.2 seconds.
Because the device sleeps immediately after Tier 5, the active energy used is incredibly small.

2. Battery Life Scenarios (Calculations)

We assume a standard 18650 Lithium-Ion Battery with a typical capacity of 3,000 mAh. Accounting for environmental factors and self-discharge, we calculate based on an effective capacity of 2,400 mAh.

Assume the device detects 2 false alarms or real alerts per day (10.4 seconds of active time per day).

Scenario A: The "Stock" Setup (No Hardware Mods)

If you build the project exactly out of the box, the ESP32-CAM board has an inefficient voltage regulator (AMS1117) and the Flame/Vibration sensors have built-in LEDs and comparator chips (LM393) that draw power constantly.

  • ESP32-CAM Unmodified Sleep: ~2.5 mA
  • Flame Sensor Module (KY-026): ~15.0 mA
  • Vibration Sensor Module (SW-420): ~15.0 mA
  • Total Sleep Current: ~32.5 mA
Average Current ≈ 32.5 mA (Sleep current completely dominates)
Battery Life = 2400 mAh / 32.5 mA = 73.8 hours
Result: ~3 Days

Scenario B: "Bare Sensor" Optimization (Easy Mod)

Instead of using the full Flame/Vibration circuit boards, you wire the bare sensor tips directly to the ESP32 pins with high-value pull-down resistors. This eliminates the LEDs and LM393 chips on the sensors.

  • ESP32-CAM Unmodified Sleep: ~2.5 mA
  • Bare Flame / Vibration sensors: ~0.05 mA
  • Total Sleep Current: ~2.55 mA
Average Current ≈ 2.55 mA
Battery Life = 2400 mAh / 2.55 mA = 941 hours
Result: ~39 Days (Over a month!)

Scenario C: Full Hardware Optimization (Professional)

To reach commercial-grade battery life, you desolder the inefficient AMS1117 voltage regulator from the ESP32-CAM and replace it with a low-dropout MCP1700 or HT7333 regulator, and desolder the red power LED.

  • ESP32 Chip (True Deep Sleep): ~0.01 mA (10 µA)
  • Efficient LDO Regulator: ~0.005 mA (5 µA)
  • Bare Sensors + Leakage: ~0.10 mA (100 µA)
  • Total Sleep Current: ~0.115 mA (115 µA)
Active Energy = (135 mA * 0.0028 hours) = 0.378 mAh/day
Sleep Energy = (0.115 mA * 24 hours) = 2.76 mAh/day
Total Daily Drain = 3.138 mAh/day
Battery Life = 2400 mAh / 3.138 mAh/day = 764 days
Result: ~2.1 Years on a single battery charge!

3. Summary for Presentations & Reports

When presenting EdgeWake, use the following summary to explain the battery strategy:

However, the software architecture guarantees that if we apply standard hardware optimizations (swapping the voltage regulator), the exact same code achieves over 2 years of surveillance on a single 18650 battery cell."
